The Caravan
Route

Oyo to Ouidah · c. 1760s

◆ ◆ ◆

Oyo dominates the region with cavalry and tribute. Dahomey is a disciplined, rising military state that still pays tribute to Oyo. Ouidah is a coastal port where European forts and African brokers run the Atlantic trade. Firearms are spreading. Standing armies and written contracts are slowly replacing freelancers. And somewhere on the road, a caravan moves toward the coast.

The World in Brief

Serious. Grounded. Human.

◆
The Big Picture

Power and Tribute

Oyo is the dominant power of the region, with the cavalry, tribute states, and political clout. Dahomey is a disciplined, rising military state that still pays tribute to Oyo. Ouidah is a coastal port under Dahomey control, where European forts and African brokers run the Atlantic trade. Firearms are spreading, and standing armies and written contracts are slowly replacing freelancers.

Tone

Serious, Grounded, Human

No monsters, no spectacle. The slave trade is the moral weight of the story, shown through people's reactions, objects, and choices rather than graphic scenes. Every character has reasons. Every choice has weight.

Everyday Details to Drop In

The texture of the world

Money
Cowrie shells, counted in strings.
Food
Yam, millet in the north, palm oil, dried fish, kola nuts.
Travel
Porters carrying loads on their heads, a few donkeys in the northern stretch, canoes on rivers.
Greetings
Respect matters. People greet elders first, and a poor greeting is an insult.

Quick Tips for Running NPCs

For the Game Master's eyes

◆
One Detail, One Line

Give each NPC one physical detail and one line of speech. That's enough to be memorable.

Let Them Want

Let NPCs want things. If the players don't engage, they still act.

Don't Show Every Secret

Reveal them through objects and reactions, not exposition.

Let Players Change NPCs

If someone shows Folarin respect, she should treat them differently later.
